SuperCal Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 26, 2026.

What we collect

  • Account details: your name and email address, and a profile picture if Apple or Google provides one, when you sign up with email or sign in with Apple or Google. For email accounts we also store your password in hashed form (we cannot read it). If you use “Hide My Email” with Apple, we only see the relay address.
  • Your content: calendars, events (titles, times, locations, notes, repeat rules), invites, friend connections, your handle, and your preferences (theme and display settings).
  • Your device calendars (optional): if you turn on “Show this device’s calendars”, SuperCal reads calendar and event details from your device to display them as a read-only overlay. This overlay stays on your device. If you explicitly choose “Copy into SuperCal”, the copied event becomes your SuperCal content, is stored and synced through our backend, and can be shared only through actions you choose. You can turn device calendar access off in SuperCal or revoke it in your system’s privacy settings.
  • Google Calendar (optional): if you connect Google Calendar, we request read-only access to the list of calendars you subscribe to and the events on calendars you can access, including shared calendars. We access your Google email address; calendar identifiers, names and colors; and event identifiers, titles, start/end dates and times, all-day status, locations and descriptions. We store your Google email address, connection time and a refresh token linked to your SuperCal account. The refresh token lets our server obtain short-lived access tokens without your Google password. We do not create, edit or delete your Google calendars or events.
  • Notifications and purchases: if you turn on notifications on iPhone or iPad, we store your device’s push-notification token so we can alert you about friend requests and invites. If you subscribe to SuperCal Plus, Apple processes the payment. Our subscription provider, RevenueCat, receives your SuperCal user ID, your purchase and transaction information (such as Apple’s purchase receipt) and technical information such as your device type and operating system, so it can confirm your subscription, and it tells us whether your subscription is active. RevenueCat also provides subscription reporting and analytics, such as purchase history and subscription performance. RevenueCat acts as our data processor; see RevenueCat’s privacy policy for details. We store that status and its expiry date with your account. We never see your card or payment details.
  • Technical data: your sign-in session and a user ID, which is also what identifies you to RevenueCat. For Sign in with Apple accounts we also keep Apple’s token so we can revoke it when you delete your account.

We do not collect your location, your phone’s address book, or advertising identifiers. (Friend connections you make inside SuperCal are stored as part of your account.) Apart from RevenueCat’s subscription reporting, we do not use third-party analytics. We have no advertising and do not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.

How we use it

Only to run SuperCal: to sign you in, store and sync your calendars across your devices, and deliver invites and shared calendars to the friends you choose.

How Google Calendar data is processed and used

Google Calendar connection is optional and is used to display Google calendars and events alongside your SuperCal plans. Our Supabase-hosted server function requests calendar lists and events from Google, processes them temporarily, and returns the display data to your signed-in app over HTTPS. The calendar overlay is held in app memory; its events are not written to our database as part of this read-only connection. Hiding a calendar changes its display in SuperCal; our server may still fetch events from Google calendars selected in your Google Calendar account.

If you explicitly choose Copy into SuperCal on an event, the copied title, time, location and description become a separate SuperCal event. That copy is stored and synced with your account and can be edited, invited or shared by you. Copying or editing the copy does not change the original Google event. Disconnecting Google does not delete copies you chose to create; you can delete those events in SuperCal.

We do not use raw, aggregated, anonymized or derived Google Calendar data for advertising, tracking, selling data, credit or lending decisions, or unrelated analytics. We do not use this data to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models, and do not transfer it to AI or machine-learning services for those purposes. RevenueCat receives subscription information as described above, not Google Calendar event contents through this integration.

SuperCal’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Google data recipients and protection

Google supplies the data and handles authorization. Supabase and its underlying infrastructure providers process connection data and temporarily process calendar responses to operate the integration. The read-only overlay is delivered to your signed-in app, not automatically shared with other SuperCal users. Copies you deliberately create and share are handled as described above. We do not sell Google data or transfer it to advertisers or data brokers.

Network connections use HTTPS/TLS. The refresh token stays on the server and is not returned to the app. Server actions require an authenticated SuperCal session. The connection table uses row-level security so normal app clients cannot directly read refresh tokens. Authorized backend services process the data to provide the feature. We do not routinely have people read Google calendar contents; any exceptional human access must be consistent with Google’s Limited Use requirements, such as your affirmative permission, security investigation, or legal obligations.

Google data retention and deletion

We keep the connection email, refresh token and connection timestamp while your Google Calendar connection exists. Calendar responses are processed temporarily for display rather than retained as an event archive on our server. The app refreshes its overlay while in use; disconnecting removes the Google overlay from the app.

Choose Disconnect next to Google Calendar in SuperCal to delete the stored connection and request token revocation from Google. Account deletion also removes the stored Google connection and attempts revocation. You can independently remove SuperCal’s access in your Google Account connections. Revocation can be affected by network or Google service failures. Google retains your original calendars and events under its own policies.

Events you explicitly copied into SuperCal remain until you delete those copies or your SuperCal account. Deleted database records may remain in provider backups until those backups expire under the provider’s retention schedule; they are not used to serve the active connection. You may contact us about deletion using the address below.

Who can see your data

Your calendars are private. An event or calendar is visible to another person only if you invite or share it with them, and only friends who accepted your request can receive invites. Your name, handle and picture are visible to other signed-in users who search for your handle.

Where it’s stored

Your data is stored with our backend provider, Supabase, on cloud infrastructure, and is protected in transit with TLS. We also use Apple for sign-in and payment processing, Google for sign-in and optional Calendar access, and RevenueCat to manage subscriptions as described above.

Deleting your data

You can delete your account at any time in Settings → Account → Delete my account. This permanently removes your profile, calendars, events, invites, friend connections and preferences, and revokes the Sign in with Apple token. Backups are purged on the provider’s normal schedule. You can also email us to request deletion.

Children

SuperCal is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect their data.
